Every pixel matters. Every color detail, every tonal gradation, every nuance you spent hours adjusting in post-processing. And yet, when you upload that photo to Instagram or any other social network, all of that disappears. Compression, cropping, algorithms that decide who sees it and who doesn't. Your work gets reduced to a 1080-pixel square that people scroll past in half a second.

![Website source code on GitHub {"priority": true, "align": "left"}](/blog/2026-09-04_por-que-construi-mi-web_codigo.png)

That frustration is what pushed me to build **my own website**. I didn't just want a template portfolio — I wanted a place where I could control every aspect of how my photographs are presented. A space where the image is the absolute protagonist, without distractions, without format limitations, and with all the technical information that I consider part of each photo's story.

## Photos the way they deserve to be seen

In [the gallery](/en/gallery) of this website, each photograph is displayed at the highest possible resolution, with a viewer that respects the original proportions and lets you enjoy every detail. But what mattered most to me was being able to accompany each image with its **real metadata**: the camera, the lens, the exposure parameters, the location, the date... All of that tells a story that social networks simply strip away.

When you take a [long exposure](/en/gallery/tags/long-exposure) of 120 seconds on the coast at dawn, the technical data isn't a _geek_ indulgence — it's part of the narrative. Knowing that the silky water required a 10-stop filter and almost two minutes of exposure changes how you look at the photo.

## Built from scratch, with care

![Photo detail with its metadata {"align": "right"}](/blog/2026-09-04_por-que-construi-mi-web_detalle.png)

The website is developed with **Next.js** and designed for performance. Images are served in optimized WebP format at multiple resolutions, loaded progressively with a dominant color _placeholder_, and the design is fully _responsive_. I don't use external image optimization services — everything is pre-processed so the experience is as fast as possible without sacrificing quality.

The dark theme is no coincidence: a neutral background is the best frame for photography, like the walls of a gallery. Every design decision serves the image.

## Not just a gallery: prints too

A photo on screen is beautiful, but a quality print on paper is something else entirely. That's why I've opened a [print store](/en/store/prints) where you can order copies of my photographs. I'm just getting started with this project, and in a future article I want to tell you all about the process: how I print at home, what papers I use, what color profiles I work with, and why I believe it's worth having a physical photo hanging on the wall.

## A living space

This website is not a finished product — it's a living project that I keep improving. I want to add more content to the blog, create tutorials on planning and editing photography, and keep refining the gallery experience. If you've made it this far, thanks for reading, and I invite you to [explore the photos](/en/gallery) at your own pace. That's how they're best enjoyed.
